02 / The issue

The journey is not
equally easy for all.

Transportation barriers are connected. A limited route can mean a longer trip; a longer trip can mean higher costs; and a late trip can mean fewer options.

01 — ACCESS

Distance becomes a barrier.

Some communities are far from schools, workplaces, hospitals, and major transport routes. People may need to walk long distances or transfer between multiple vehicles just to reach a destination.

02 — COST

Small fares add up.

Daily fares may seem manageable when viewed as a single trip. But repeated rides, transfers, and return journeys can become a significant monthly expense—especially for students and minimum-wage earners.

03 — AVAILABILITY

Not every route runs all day.

In some areas, transport becomes harder to find during evenings, weekends, or off-peak hours. People who finish work or school late may have fewer ways to get home.

04 — SAFETY & RELIABILITY

Waiting is part of the journey.

Poor lighting, limited shelters, unclear route information, inaccessible sidewalks, and unpredictable arrival times can make commuting stressful and difficult.

Important: These are issue areas for the advocacy, not claims that every community experiences them in the same way. Local surveys, interviews, and official data should be used to describe a specific place accurately.
